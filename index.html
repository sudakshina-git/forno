FORNO. A pizzeria that holds the heat.

I invented a Copenhagen pizzeria, built its brand, directed a 46-second winter film with AI image and video tools, and designed the system that lets its agency and its staff run a new campaign every season.

01

A restaurant doesn't need one campaign. It needs a year of them.

Seasons, festivals, local events, holidays: a restaurant has something to say almost every month, and each moment needs design.

The brief: an agency builds the brand and the big campaigns, and gives the restaurant a system to keep everyday content on-brand in between. The agency stays a creative partner all year.

This project explores one of those triggers: the seasons.

The client is invented: FORNO, a neighbourhood pizzeria in Copenhagen. December is produced in full; three lighter seasons show the system repeating.

03

Branding: two directions.

One playful and fully illustrated; one calm and editorial, keeping only the best of the first.

First approach: an illustrated neighbourhood pizzeria

Oil-pastel characters, a striped awning, Nyhavn everywhere. Warm, but busy, and close to many other pizza brands.

Second approach: a calm restaurant with one illustrated touch

Two things stayed: the hand-painted wordmark and one character, the rider. Everything else became calm and editorial: 80 per cent restraint, 20 per cent illustrated warmth.

04

Nine Christmas stories. Four of them became one film.

December, explored as an ad film. Nine stories, each weighed against credits and what the tools could do well.

  1. 1
    Is It Here Yet?A child keeps asking if the pizza has arrived. Each question cuts to where it is now: leaving the oven, crossing the canal, turning onto her street.
  2. 2
    Follow the BoxThe box is the hero. From the moment the lid closes, the camera never leaves it: counter, carrier, bicycle, canal, stairwell, table.
  3. 3
    Crumbs KnowsAn old dog waits by the door every evening. The family thinks it's waiting for them. It's waiting for the FORNO crusts.
  4. 4
    Before the OvenA chef leaves the kitchen to gather the Christmas menu: flour, cheese, tomatoes, herbs. Flour dust turns to snow; tomato red turns to FORNO red.
  5. 5
    Wait — Pineapple?A fast, comic relay of ingredients racing to the oven, until a child's voice stops everything with one last request: pineapple.
  6. 6
    The Metro SliceA metro carriage full of tinsel and Christmas jumpers. One passenger carries a warm FORNO box, and one by one everyone stops looking at the decorations.
  7. 7
    A Table for Those StayingNot everyone goes home for Christmas. FORNO sets a long table for people spending December in Copenhagen away from family. One guest hesitates at the door, then comes in.
  8. 8
    Close Enough to TasteNo plot, only appetite. Extreme close-ups of dough, fire, cheese and steam, with sound doing half the work.
  9. 9
    The Longest Way HomeCopenhagen at the end of a December day: people heading home by bus, bike and metro, while one hot pizza crosses the same city.

The film combines 1, 2 and 9, with 8 for close-ups: a child waits at a window while a hot FORNO box crosses a cold December city. The box is the only warm thing in it. Hence the title, Hold the Heat.

06

The method I settled into.

Most of the work was learning why a frame failed. Six habits came out of it.

1Stills first, motion last.

Every shot locked as a still before animating. Magnific's select-and-edit fixed details without starting over.

2AI, Canva and my own hands.

AI made the pictures; type, layout and the real logo, added in Canva and by hand, finished them.

3One model for each job.

Seedream Pro for stills, Kling for video. Switching models changed faces and light.

485 per cent cold, 15 warm.

Mostly cold blue, one warm source, in every prompt. Forty shots, one night.

5Camera moves, named and few.

"Truck right", "push in": one simple named move per shot.

6Hands, kept simple.

Still the hardest part. Close-ups only, one simple action. Still learning.

07

What didn't work, and what it taught me.

Frames and clips that didn't make it, and what changed because of them.

Cut
The handoff at the door

Many tries. Blurred faces, crowded doorways, a courier inside the hallway.

The arrival happens on the table instead. We never see the door open.

Rejected
The rider who looked like he was smoking

"Camera static" on a moving cyclist made him stop and exhale. It read as smoking.

Travel language and a named camera move.

Rejected
The family dinner

Several models, many prompts. Always a stock Christmas photo.

Shoot from directly above. Hands and plates tell the story; faces turn away.

Rejected
The table in motion

Within a second, faces drifted and hands merged into cutlery.

A slow push-in on the still, done in DaVinci.

Rejected
The model wrote its own logo

It invented a sign that isn't the brand.

The real mark goes on afterwards, in Canva.

Rejected
Hands

As soon as anyone held something, fingers multiplied.

Hands only in close-ups with one simple action: lifting a slice.

Explored
Could the whole film be drawn?

Lovely, but it made FORNO a children's brand.

Illustration stays on the packaging. The film is photographic.

Rejected
The delivery, animated

Funny for the wrong reasons.

Cut. The courier's face is never shown; he only carries the heat.

Cut
Beautiful aerials that said nothing

Gorgeous, with no story job.

Three aerials kept, each one moving the rider forward.

Turning point
The first frame that worked

Rough, but the mood was right. Everything after was measured against it.

11

What I did, and what the tools did.

Researched, written, directed and edited by me; images and motion produced with AI.

Me

  • The brief, the research and the restaurant itself
  • The brand direction, and the decision to drop the first one
  • Nine story ideas and the one that was chosen
  • Storyboards, shot list and every prompt
  • Choosing, rejecting and fixing each frame
  • The edit, the pacing, the sound and the logo reveal
  • The menu, the copy and the prototype's design

The tools

  • Magnific: Seedream Pro for stills, Kling for video, Hailuo for early video trials, all from my references and prompts
  • DaVinci Resolve: edit, grade, sound, push-ins
  • Canva: type and layout for posters and menus
  • Claude: thinking partner, copy drafts and the prototype's code
  • ChatGPT: second opinions and research

What I learned

Story and direction are a craft of their own, and what I'm studying next.

Real references beat long prompts.

Quality follows budget. The film is 720p, made on a small credit budget; more credits would mean higher resolution.

Hands are still unsolved.
